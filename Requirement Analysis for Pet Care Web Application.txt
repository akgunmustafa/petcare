1. Identifying Stakeholders

    Pet Owners (End Users): They use the website to find vets, call pet taxis, book pet hotels/sitters, and buy pet supplies.

    Service Providers:

        Veterinarians: Answer questions, give online consultations, and take clinic appointments.

        Pet Taxi Drivers: Transport pets safely from one place to another.

        Pet Hotels & Individual Sitters: Take care of pets for money when owners are away.

    Suppliers / Pet Shops: Sell pet food and basic equipment on the platform.

    Project Team & Admins: Developers, UI/UX designers, and managers who build the website, check user documents, and keep the system safe.

2. Gathering Business Requirements

    How we gather information: We use online surveys with pet owners and short interviews with vets and pet sitters.

    What is the purpose of the website?
    It is an all-in-one service and community platform for pets. It brings together veterinary care, pet transport (pet taxi), pet sitting/hotels, and pet supplies in one place.

    Who is the target audience?

        Age: 18 to 65 years old.

        Profile: Pet owners, animal lovers, vets, and pet care providers.

        Tech Level: Basic smartphone and internet users (the design must be very simple for everyone).

    Are there any special functions?

        One-Click Emergency Button: Quickly finds the nearest open vet and calls a pet ambulance.

        Live GPS Tracking: Shows the pet taxi location on a live map.

        Pet Hotel & Sitter Booking: Allows users to rent a room in a pet hotel or hire a trusted individual sitter.

        Ask a Vet (Q&A): A forum where owners ask questions and verified vets answer.

        Digital Pet Card: Keeps vaccine dates and medical history in the user profile.

3. Defining Functional Requirements

    User & Pet Profile System: Users can sign up, log in, and create a digital health card for their pets (age, breed, vaccines).

    Vet Q&A and Appointment: Users can upload photos to ask questions to vets, book clinic visits, or make video calls.

    Pet Taxi Booking: Users can select start and end locations on a map, see the price, and call a vehicle for their pet.

    Pet Hotel & Sitter Reservation (New Module): Users can filter hotels or home sitters by date, price, and location, read reviews, and book a stay. Sitters can send daily photos and videos to the owner.

    Needs & Solidarity Board: Users can buy pet food/equipment or post ads for lost pets, adoption, and urgent blood needs.

    Safe Payment & Admin Panel: Money is kept safely in a pool (escrow) until the service is finished. Admins approve vet diplomas and sitter IDs.

4. Identifying Non-Functional Requirements

    Usability (HCI Focus): The interface must be clean, simple, and stress-free. In an emergency, a panicked pet owner must find the "Emergency Button" in 1 second (big red button, clear icons).

    Performance: Pages must load in less than 2 seconds. Map and GPS tracking must work in real time without delay.

    Accessibility: The website must follow WCAG standards (good color contrast, readable font sizes, and screen reader support).

    Security & Privacy: All user data and credit card payments must be protected with SSL encryption. Sitter and vet identities must be verified.

    Scalability & Responsiveness: The design must be Mobile-First (works perfectly on phones, tablets, and desktops) and support thousands of active users at the same time.

5. Creating User Scenarios and Use Cases

    Scenario 1: Calling an Emergency Pet Taxi

        User: A pet owner whose dog is sick at night and who has no car.

        Action: The user opens the app and clicks the "Emergency" button.

        System Support: The website finds the nearest open vet clinic, calls a pet taxi to the user's home, and sends a notification to the vet.

    Scenario 2: Booking a Pet Sitter & Transport

        User: A cat owner going on a 3-day business trip.

        Action: The user goes to the "Pet Hotel & Sitter" module, selects dates, chooses a verified home sitter, and checks the "Add Pet Taxi" box.

        System Support: The system sends the cat's digital vaccine card to the sitter, processes the payment safely, and schedules a pet taxi for pickup.

6. Prototyping and Mockups

    We create low-fidelity wireframes and interactive prototypes using Figma.

    The prototype shows the Site Map (Dashboard, Emergency, Vet, Pet Taxi, Hotel/Sitter, Supplies, Profile) and basic navigation buttons.

    We show these screens to 5 pet owners and 2 vets to test if the menus are easy to understand before coding.

7. Preparing the Requirement Document

    We write a clear Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document that includes all steps above.

    In-Scope (Proje Dahili): Web app design, vet Q&A, pet taxi calling, pet hotel/sitter booking, and basic pet supplies.

    Out-of-Scope (Proje Dışı): Selling prescription medical drugs (illegal online) and physical hardware production (like GPS collars).

8. Validation and Approval of Requirements

    We organize a final review meeting with stakeholders (pet owners, vets,sitters, and the project instructor).

    We check if all user needs and HCI usability rules are met.

    After fixing small errors, stakeholders formally approve the document, and we start the design/coding phase.
